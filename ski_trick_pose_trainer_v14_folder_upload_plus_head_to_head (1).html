How automatic trick separation works

The detector looks for sustained changes in torso angle, shoulder/hip orientation, ski angle, and normalized joint motion. It merges nearby high-motion moments into one candidate trick. This is automatic, but it is not perfect: carving, crashes, camera cuts, rails, or very subtle tricks can create false starts. The sensitivity control lets you tune this per video.

What this version learns

Every time step contains normalized joint positions, joint velocities, visibility, body-orientation features, and estimated left/right ski orientation. The LSTM receives those time steps in order. Classes are balanced during training so a trick with more uploaded clips does not receive more influence simply because of its count. After a prediction, your confirmation or correction can be added as a labelled example and the model retrains automatically.
Ski tracking note: the skis are estimated from ankle, heel, and foot landmarks rather than directly detected at their physical tips/tails. For this version, reprocess original videos instead of importing older pose datasets because the model now has extra ski features.
Important limitations

This is still an experimental browser prototype. A skier who is tiny, heavily occluded, motion-blurred, or fully inverted can be hard for a general human pose model to track. Camera viewpoint also affects what 2D pose can reveal. For best results, use clips containing one visible skier and trim them so the trick fills most of the clip. The app tracks the body, not the physical ski tips/tails.
